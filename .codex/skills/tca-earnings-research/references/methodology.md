# Research method

## Company and IPO work

1. Identify issuer, business segments, reporting interval and public cutoff. Group earnings release, filed exhibits, call materials and formal financial report as one event with distinct document versions. IPOs may have no ticker/history; retain stable issuer identity and mark unavailable periods.
2. Compare comparable periods: organic revenue, volume/price/mix, margins, cash conversion, working capital and forward operating indicators. Programs calculate values; inspect original notes where tags/periods are ambiguous. Do not compare cumulative cash flow with standalone quarterly cash flow or combine currencies.
3. Attribute changes to demand, pricing, capacity, costs, acquisitions, accounting changes or share gains. Compare management language with previous disclosure, not just keyword frequency.
4. Seek contradiction: cancellations, duplicate orders, customer budget cuts, inventories/receivables outrunning demand, nonrecurring gains, dependence on one customer, rapid capacity additions. Missing disclosure is not proof of deterioration or improvement.
5. Link operating change to segment and industry theme with source support. Preserve new-theme proposals separately from approved universe mappings.
6. State what changed, competing explanation, evidence quality, falsification condition and the next observable test. Use no price target or deterministic buy/sell conclusion.

IPO supplements: customer concentration, revenue recognition, related parties, unit economics, cohort evidence, financing dependence and use of proceeds. TAM estimates and sponsor narratives are claims, not verified demand. Registration amendments may be material; public release is the historical availability boundary.

## Industry quarterly review

Freeze the versioned core sample and actual reporting-period mapping. Report expected issuers, disclosed, fetched and researched counts separately. Include flat/negative companies and unmatched supply-chain links. Reread originals behind decisive claims, and inspect a budgeted sample of names not selected by daily screening.

Assess magnitude, breadth, durability, customer demand, bottlenecks and profit capture. Repeated news derived from one call counts as one underlying source. Several suppliers exposed to one customer are not independent end-demand confirmation. Separate data completeness from inference strength.

Challenge the thesis before synthesis. Each challenge must be accepted, rejected with evidence, or retained unresolved. The strongest justified outcome may be insufficient_data or no new theme. Set next-quarter operating tests; revisions preserve earlier judgments and cutoff.

## Historical assessment

Use contemporaneously available document versions and sample membership, including failures/delistings when available. Never evaluate using later amendments unknowingly. Models may know subsequent events from training: require provided evidence for factual claims and label retrospective limitations. Prospective thesis outcomes matter more than a persuasive historical narrative. Share-price performance is optional follow-up, not proof of operating-thesis accuracy.
